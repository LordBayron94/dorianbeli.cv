About

I am a doctoral researcher at the Department of Computing, University of Turku, working on context-aware OCR and handwritten text recognition (HTR) for historical documents. My position is funded as a Marie Skłodowska-Curie (MSCA COFUND) fellowship within the HAIF doctoral programme — Human-Centric Artificial Intelligence for a Sustainable Future.

My research asks how vision-language models can be used to interpret archival material faithfully, transparently and reproducibly — moving beyond line-level recognition towards page- and document-level understanding, while remaining accountable to visual evidence, document structure and historical context. Historical records are not merely containers of words: their meaning is shaped by columns, neighbouring entries, handwriting styles and record-level conventions, and trustworthy AI for cultural heritage has to respect that.

Before moving into research I worked in industry as a data engineer, building and operating data pipelines and infrastructure. That engineering background — reproducibility, evaluation, systems that hold up in production — carries directly into how I approach research.

Research

Power of AI for Public Engagement HAIF0002 · 2026 – 2030

Doctoral project on trustworthy AI for cultural heritage: using vision-language models for context-aware OCR/HTR of historical documents. The work focuses on evaluating model outputs against visual evidence, document structure and historical fidelity — making hallucinations, over-corrections, reading-order failures and record-level inconsistencies visible instead of hiding them behind fluent transcriptions.

Supervised by Prof. Filip Ginter · Co-funded by the European Union (Horizon Europe, MSCA grant agreement No. 101177564).
